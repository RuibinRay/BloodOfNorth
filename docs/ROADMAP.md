## Immediate next steps

1. Play Acts II to V by hand (main menu, Start at an Act) and say where they are unfair, unclear, too easy or too slow: the raid, Kettil, the shore battle, Arne, Ulf, the fights on the Sea-Raven's deck, Rurik, the tannery yard and Skarde first.
2. Rule on the items under "Under review" in the story bible (Act I's Bjornstead is still the old farm in the dale).
3. Supply the Higgsfield key ID and secret, run `npm run hf -- auth`, then generate the key art and reference sheets.
4. Decide where the project lives: a PC that can run UE5, or continue in the browser for Act VI.

## Known gaps in 0.17

- **Nothing in Act V has been played by a person.** Its numbers are first guesses, in one table (`A5` in `src/data/act5.js`).
- **The rebuilding is three timed tasks.** The story lists what the tower, the harbor and the ship each cost without giving Eirik a choice between them, so he builds all three and is told each cost. There is no stockpile and nothing to weigh.
- **The hunger winter is a walk through its scenes.** Counting grain, digging the latrine and burning the straw are one key each or a short timed task; the quarrel between Hrefna and Ingrid, the blot and both votes are told. The sacrifice is not shown. Nobody visibly starves or sickens beyond two pallets by the barn.
- **The whale is one large shape** with an opened flank, and the work on her is three timed cuts.
- **The hunting camp is small and its stealth is light.** The story's thirty guards are three who walk, three by the fire and five who come at the end. There is one detection meter; being seen sends Eirik back to the last objective. The pits are a wait of about half a minute while the dogs pass. The eleven prisoners follow a set path. The black raven's "pictures" are a dark filter over the view and three marks to find, each with a label of the game's own.
- **The supply ships cannot be burned**, which is the story's course: Eirik lets them sail.
- **Gannet Rock and Vængr are still the small sandbox islands.** The march to the third village is a hundred metres, the forest settlement is five log houses, a dozen berserkers stand for sixty, and Svanhild's thirty guards are three.
- **Skarde's duel is a real fight with one scripted turn**: at four-fifths of his health he says the lie, Eirik is thrown down, gets up and waits, for about half a minute that is watched, not played. It can be lost afterwards and is then retried.
- **The voyages are cards**, and the deck on the way home stands still in the water.
- **Torvald's cell is a log cage beside the wall**, because buildings cannot be entered. His confession is the story's text, read through.
- **The archery contest and the kiss at the harbor wall are cutscenes.** The contest runs about a minute and can be skipped.
- Astrid's shoulder, Bjorn's leg and Svanhild's seasickness are in their scenes and not carried as states.
- Act V begun from the menu assumes Acts I to IV: the mail shirt, the first reforging, the token.
- The sandbox's fight with Skarde (the Wolf's Table) is separate from the story's.

## Known gaps carried over from 0.16

- **The ships do not sail.** At sea and in Hedeby Sound the Sea-Raven's deck stands still in the water. Leif's seamanship, the oars snapped across a longship's bow, the steering through the rocks and the ramming are told, with a sound and a shake. Voyages are cards.
- **The sea fights are stand-ins.** Six men of Ravenhold stand for ten, six guards of Solvik for forty, eight boarders for three ships' crews. Torvald's fight on the knarr, Astrid at the masthead and the fire buckets are seen, not played. At Hedeby the convoy of twenty ships and eight warships is the Sea-Raven and half a dozen boats, against two longships.
- **Rurik's duel is a real fight with two scripted turns** (he knows the axe at about three-quarters of his health; Eirik slips at a little under half, and Rurik waits). It can be lost and is then retried from the last objective. His men's surrender is told.
- **Wessex is small.** About a dozen houses inside the earth bank, a church, the reeve's hall, one farm. Buildings cannot be entered: the feverish girl lies on a pallet by the farm door and Wulfstan stands outside his hall. The townspeople have four stock phrases of something like Old English. The land ends a couple of hundred metres behind the town.
- **Kinn, Fedje, Skorpa and Gulen are still the small sandbox islands** (60 to 150 m across), so Chapter 5 is five short scenes, not five missions: three people pulled from the water, a fifty-metre trail and five looters, a tower held against nine, three wounded carried thirty paces each on the grey horse, two spells of digging. Jarnøy, which the story says was struck too, is not visited. "What they see" is narration.
- **The council stands before Bjorn's longhouse**, not in it. Listening to the six kings is walking up to each.
- **Hedeby Sound's tasks are timed strokes and marks**: two ropes, three rocks, three people in the water, six boarders. Nobody can be lost. Ari, Astrid's shoulder and the ramming are scenes.
- **The confrontation with Torvald is two key presses** (swing, lower), because the story gives Eirik no choice there.
- **Kettil's raid on Ravenhold is told at the gate**, as the story tells it: it happens while Eirik is away.
- Wounds do not last: Astrid's shoulder and Bjorn's leg are shown in their scenes and not carried forward yet.
- The sandbox's fight with Rurik (the Wolf's Table) is separate from the story's: beating him in one does not count in the other.
- Act IV starts with a mail shirt and the first reforging whether or not Act III was played, when it is begun from the menu.

## Known gaps carried over from 0.15

- A few lines the story tells without dialogue were given to a speaker (Kettil's demand, the lawspeakers, the drunk sailor, Leif's tall tale). Each is built from the story's own sentence and marked `(game)` in `src/data/act2.js` where the wording is new.

## Known gaps carried over from 0.12

- This is still a procedural, low-polygon build in a browser. There are no skin textures, hair strands, motion capture or recorded voices, and it does not look like the games the brief names. Faces are built from simple shapes; mouths follow the letters of a line, not a voice.
- Cloth and hair swing as single stiff pieces. Foot planting bends the legs on slopes; it does not stop feet sliding at every speed.
- Blocking is still a facing check with an impact reaction, not weapon-against-weapon collision.
- Mud, rain, destructible objects and breakable shields from the brief are not built. Lighting and materials are unchanged.
- None of it has been played by hand; elite difficulty is unknown.


- Skills are rules and numbers, not new moves: none adds an animation or a new button except Rally. There is no way to unlearn one.
- The point rate (one per 150 renown) is a guess: a full run of what is built gives roughly twenty points against forty-five to buy everything.


- Crew can be wounded but cannot die and never leave: no wages, no morale. How fast they go down (about half a minute unaided) is a guess.
- All melee weapons share the same three swing animations (the spear has a thrust, the long axe a two-handed chop). No weapon upgrades, no bows beyond the boy's, and the story chapters use neither the new weapons nor the new enemy kinds.
- Weapon numbers were set by measurement against a held target, not by fighting with them. Which weapon is best, and whether any is useless, is unknown.
- Sea battles are one ship against one. No fleets, no wind, no fire arrows, no enemy ramming; hostile ships ignore the rowing boat and do not land.
- Hostile ships only block the Sea-Raven; traders can still be sailed through.
- In a boarding fight both decks are flat rectangles: benches and the mast do not block anyone.
- Sea battles were validated by script, with stand-in blows for the player. How hard they are for a person is unknown.
- Crew ashore have no path-finding and can snag on rocks; boarding the ship brings them back at once.
- The ship, the horn and the raid were validated by script, not by hand. Crew strength in particular is a guess.

- The sea is calm and the wind always fair: no weather, no currents, no swimming, no capsizing. The sail is a speed toggle.
- The islands are sandbox content, not story: a handful of people, no buildings, no routines. Three are landmarks only.
- The Left Brother is handed out ahead of its act (the design places it in Act IV) to prove the omen-to-weapon chain. It adds damage; it has no moves of its own.
- Sailing, landing and the island fights were validated by script, not by hand.
- The horse cannot be carried by boat, so it stays in the home valley.

- Houses cannot be entered.
- No voice acting; music and effects are synthesised.
- Villagers hold a post and change once in the evening; they do not react to weapons or theft.
- The camera can clip through roofs at steep angles near buildings.
- NPC steering has no path-finding; scripted routes are hand-placed to avoid obstacles.
- Stealth, aiming, the wolf and the ice were validated by scripted input, not by hand. The Chapter 2
  tracker section is probably too forgiving: a cautious scripted player was never noticed.
- Chapter 2 reuses the Hrafnvik valley; the wolf can only be shot or fed (no scaring it off with fire yet).
- Snares and fever from the chapter outline are not built.
- Chapter 3: the ravens are simple shapes; trees keep their autumn colour in spring; Raven's Eye was flown by script, not by hand.
- Act II combat has one melee weapon (Ragnar's axe) and the bow; no shield for the player, no executions, no weapon classes yet. Balance was set against scripted players, not people.
- Adult Eirik and the raiders are the same placeholder rig; attacks reuse three swing animations.
- The open-world sandbox is one district with two small islands inside the existing fjord. It has no story, no ships, no second district, no theft, and no feuds.
- In the sandbox, people Eirik killed are alive again after loading a save; only marks, standing, silver and the horse and boat positions are stored.
- Riding and rowing were driven by script. The horse has no path-finding and stops at obstacles; you cannot fight from the saddle.
- A manual save during the night or a hunt restores to the last checkpoint, not the exact position.
