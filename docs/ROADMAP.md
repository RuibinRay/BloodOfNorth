## Bodies (built on the branch `bodies` from main after the faces)

Asked for: better bodies, like the faces. Built: `src/entities/bodies.js` builds everything below the head from the
look (the torso, arms, hands, legs, boots, skirt or dress, cloak, apron, mail); each part is a skin lofted through the
rings of a profile and bends on two joints (the waist, the elbow, the knee, the fingers' middle joints), the way the jaw
and hanging hair do in heads.js. The fingers and thumbs are in the mesh drawn only near (like the face), so a distant
crowd costs no more than before; the body itself costs about 150 more triangles a figure (the Black Shore battle
draws 731k of its 750k budget, up from 724k). New look fields: `heft` (how heavy all over), `belly`, `bust`, `stoop`
(from age if not given), set for about thirty-five people from their portraits. A held thing sits in the fist, about
three centimetres further down and in than it used to. Seen in still pictures from the front, side, back and three
quarters, standing, walking and in ten poses; `npm run check` passes.

Known gaps:
- **Judged from still pictures**: no one has watched the fingers close, a skirt swing or a cloak sway in play.
- **The shoulder does not blend into the chest**: the deltoid is part of the arm, so with an arm raised high the top
  of the torso shows a corner under it.
- **Long hair, cloaks and arms can still pass through each other**, and a cloak hangs straight down when he sits.
- **The wind** (added after the bodies): hair that hangs down the back, cloaks and skirts are moved by the weather's
  wind (`shared.uWind`, the same strength that moves the trees and the flags), always from one direction (toward +x, as the snow drifts), in gusts with a
  flutter; a cloak now trails behind a runner (it used to swing forward, a sign that was the wrong way round). Short
  hair, beards and braids lying on the chest do not move in it, and it blows indoors as well as out. A long dress
  follows the thighs further than a tunic, so it lies over the lap when she sits, and opens a little at the front and
  back seams in a long stride.
- **The rank and file of the battle lines keep their own simpler figures.**

## Faces and hair (built on the branch `faces-and-hair` from 0.29; merged into main)

Asked for: better eyes, noses, ears, mouths and above all hair, and every important character looking like themselves.
Built: `src/entities/heads.js` builds every head from its look (the skull, nose, ears, eyes, lids, brows, lips, jaw,
hair and beard; the fields it reads are listed at its top); `humanoid.js` skins a vertex to two joints where a piece
bends (jaw, mouth corners, hair that hangs onto the back, a long beard or a braid lying on the chest), and animates the
jaw, lids, irises and mouth corners. Looks for about eighty named people in the cast tables, `champions.js` and
`player.js`, matched to their portraits. Seen in labelled sheets of every style and of the cast, from the front and
three-quarters, and in scenes; `npm run check` passes.

Known gaps:
- **Judged from still pictures**, like the animation pass: no one has watched a face talk, blink or glance in play.
- **In play the camera is four or five metres behind Eirik**, so faces are read in conversation (the close-ups, tightened
  to head and shoulders after the new faces) and in cutscenes; at play distance what tells people apart is their hair,
  beard, colour and build.
- **Hair is a shaped shell with locks, a hanging curtain and tubes for braids**, not strands: it does not move in the
  wind, and long hair can pass through a raised arm or a cloak's collar.
- **Long beards and braids lying on the chest bend between the head and the chest**: with the head turned far they
  kink at the neck.
- **Eyes and mouths have shapes now, not likenesses**: on top of size, spacing, colour, lids and brows, an eye can be
  round, narrow, hooded, deep-set, or lifted or drooping at the outer corner, and a mouth can have a cupid's bow, a
  full lower lip, a crooked corner, or corners that turn up or down at rest (`eyeShape`, `mouthShape`, `corners`), set
  for about thirty people from what their portraits say of their eyes and mouths. Eirik's Appearance page does not offer
  them yet. Unnamed folk, raiders and the rank and file keep the default face (the rank and file their own simpler one).
- **A portrait and the story disagree in two places, left as the portraits have it**: Liv's eyes are her father's blue
  (the story gives her her mother's), and Håkon in Act VII keeps his black hair streaked with grey, not the grey
  hair and long beard his look had before.

## Known gaps in 0.29

- **The shield walls have been fought through by a script with real health** (`wallFight()` in `tools/check/page.js`, at three levels of skill taken from the champion-fight bot). It stands in the line, braces at their horn, sounds its own when they reel, cuts at their front rank, guards against their thrusts, raises its shield when fire arrows come at it, and turns on the men who break through. Results, in seconds and the health he ended on:
  - **a good player** holds Iron Shore (85 s, 48 left) and breaks both Black Shore lines (47 s, 79 left; 118 s, 21 left);
  - **an average player** holds Iron Shore (91 s, 18 to 28 left) and breaks both Black Shore lines (75 to 81 s, 65 to 77 left);
  - **a novice dies in all three**, mostly to their thrusts over the rim (about 100 of his health at Iron Shore) and the men who come through.
  - **One fix came out of it**: the breath a raised shield costs (added at the owner's request) was so steep that raising it for their horn left him too winded to brace when the rush came, and every player died. A shield now costs nothing for its first 2.6 seconds up (`guardGrace`), so raising it for the horn or against one thrust is free and only a shield held up from rush to rush drains him. Holding it up the whole time now costs 108 to 117 health a wall, more than he has.
  - **Not decided**: whether a novice should die at all three. The thrust over the rim (9 health, a 0.55 s warning) is what kills him; a longer warning would help slow hands. The check runs the average player and fails if he dies.

- **Everything is in one place again, except what is still being made.** `act7-fight-tuning` (Ulf, Kettil and Vigdis made to last longer, from a bot playtest) and `story-hints-stay` (one-time tips no longer wipe the story's hints) were merged here, and `main` was brought up from 0.16 to this. Both were made in other sessions from 0.22 and were checked there, not replayed here: here every chapter starts and the checks pass with them in. A fifth branch, `faces-and-hair`, is being worked on in another session as this is written (it has uncommitted changes to `src/entities/humanoid.js` and `src/world/geo.js`, cut from the animation pass before the fighting moves were added): it is not merged, and will meet the fighting moves in the same file when it is.

- **Movement was judged from still pictures.** A film strip shows eight moments of a stride side by side; it cannot show whether the walk feels right at speed, whether the bank into a turn is too much, or whether a head that nods is charming or silly. Nobody has watched any of it run, and a person has not seen it at all.
- **What was not touched**: the blows themselves and the bow (worked on in 0.19), faces and speech, crouching, swimming, the hare's hop, fish, and the ships' own motion on the water.
- **The fighting moves that were redone** (guard, block with a shield, roll, sidestep, the falls) were looked at in film strips of one figure standing alone, not in a fight. A roll goes over forwards or backwards by which way he is facing when he starts it; sideways he only tucks. Whether a full turn in 0.42 seconds reads as a roll or a blur at speed, nobody has seen. Only men cut down in a fight fall forward or half turned: anyone the story lays down still falls onto his back, because cameras look at them there.
- **The lean, the bank, the nod and the tail are read from what moves the figure**, not told to it: someone teleported or turned on the spot by a script banks for a moment as if he had turned.
- **A hound and a wolf wag alike**, and every horse swishes its tail the same way.
- **The rank and file step in place when the line creeps**: their legs follow how far the line moves, not their own feet on the ground.

- **Every person who speaks in a conversation now has a face** (Ketil and Kol were the last two). About thirty more cast members (Hallgerd, Ottar the falconer, the priest in Wessex, the frog-cure merchant and others) have no portrait, because they never speak in the conversation box, only in a caption or not at all, so a portrait would show nowhere. Kol's portrait has a short beard where his figure has stubble.

## Known gaps carried over from 0.12

- This is still a procedural, low-polygon build in a browser. There are no skin textures, hair strands, motion capture or recorded voices, and it does not look like the games the brief names. Faces are built from simple shapes; mouths follow the letters of a line, not a voice.
- Cloth and hair swing as single stiff pieces. Foot planting bends the legs on slopes; it does not stop feet sliding at every speed.
- Blocking is still a facing check with an impact reaction, not weapon-against-weapon collision.
- Mud, rain, destructible objects and breakable shields from the brief are not built. Lighting and materials are unchanged.
- None of it has been played by hand; elite difficulty is unknown.


- Skills are rules and numbers, not new moves: none adds an animation or a new button except Rally. There is no way to unlearn one.
- The point rate (one per 150 renown) is a guess: a full run of what is built gives roughly twenty points against forty-five to buy everything.


- Crew can be wounded but cannot die and never leave: no wages, no morale. How fast they go down (about half a minute unaided) is a guess.
- All melee weapons share the same three swing animations (the spear has a thrust, the long axe a two-handed chop). No weapon upgrades, no bows beyond the boy's, and the story chapters use neither the new weapons nor the new enemy kinds.
- Weapon numbers were set by measurement against a held target, not by fighting with them. Which weapon is best, and whether any is useless, is unknown.
- Sea battles are one ship against one. No fleets, no wind, no fire arrows, no enemy ramming; hostile ships ignore the rowing boat and do not land.
- Hostile ships only block the Sea-Raven; traders can still be sailed through.
- In a boarding fight both decks are flat rectangles: benches and the mast do not block anyone.
- Sea battles were validated by script, with stand-in blows for the player. How hard they are for a person is unknown.
- Crew ashore have no path-finding and can snag on rocks; boarding the ship brings them back at once.
- The ship, the horn and the raid were validated by script, not by hand. Crew strength in particular is a guess.

- The sea is calm and the wind always fair: no weather, no currents, no swimming, no capsizing. The sail is a speed toggle.
- The islands are sandbox content, not story: a handful of people, no buildings, no routines. Three are landmarks only.
- The Left Brother is handed out ahead of its act (the design places it in Act IV) to prove the omen-to-weapon chain. It adds damage; it has no moves of its own.
- Sailing, landing and the island fights were validated by script, not by hand.
- The horse cannot be carried by boat, so it stays in the home valley.

- Houses cannot be entered.
- No voice acting; music and effects are synthesised.
- Villagers hold a post and change once in the evening; they do not react to weapons or theft.
- The camera can clip through roofs at steep angles near buildings.
- NPC steering has no path-finding; scripted routes are hand-placed to avoid obstacles.
- Stealth, aiming, the wolf and the ice were validated by scripted input, not by hand. The Chapter 2
  tracker section is probably too forgiving: a cautious scripted player was never noticed.
- Chapter 2 reuses the Hrafnvik valley; the wolf can only be shot or fed (no scaring it off with fire yet).
- Snares and fever from the chapter outline are not built.
- Chapter 3: the ravens are simple shapes; trees keep their autumn colour in spring; Raven's Eye was flown by script, not by hand.
- Act II combat has one melee weapon (Ragnar's axe) and the bow; no shield for the player, no executions, no weapon classes yet. Balance was set against scripted players, not people.
- Adult Eirik and the raiders are the same placeholder rig; attacks reuse three swing animations.
- The open-world sandbox is one district with two small islands inside the existing fjord. It has no story, no ships, no second district, no theft, and no feuds.
- In the sandbox, people Eirik killed are alive again after loading a save; only marks, standing, silver and the horse and boat positions are stored.
- Riding and rowing were driven by script. The horse has no path-finding and stops at obstacles; you cannot fight from the saddle.
- A manual save during the night or a hunt restores to the last checkpoint, not the exact position.
